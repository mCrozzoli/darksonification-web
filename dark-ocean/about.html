Dark Ocean

The North Atlantic, November 2020 – July 2023, read by eye and by sound.

Each cell of the map is one place on an ocean model grid, carrying temperature, salinity and thirteen biogeochemical variables. Colour shows either the water mass a cell belongs to or a continuous field. Press ♪ to hear the whole region on the date shown. Time is the ground: temperature becomes pitch (the coldest date on screen is the lowest note, the warmest the highest), salinity becomes space, nutrients become rain, anomaly (how unusual the date is for each place against the whole record) becomes tremolo, and the most common water mass holds a bed tone and rings a clean bell when another mass takes over. Space is the figure: the place you pick chimes on the dates it is upwelling or at a productive front. Cells you trace sound together as a chord.

Press L for the legend: hold a chip to hear one change on the date shown, even with ♪ off. The sound tab has a fader for every layer. Headphones help.

Data

  • Ocean fields: a gridded reanalysis of the North Atlantic from the E.U. Copernicus Marine Service. Generated using E.U. Copernicus Marine Service Information.
  • Water masses follow the end-member framework for the waters around Iceland and the Denmark Strait: Mastropole, D., et al. (2017). On the hydrography of Denmark Strait. Journal of Geophysical Research: Oceans, 122. doi:10.1002/2016JC012007
  • Coastlines: Natural Earth (public domain).

Sound

The sound is made by SuperCollider running inside this page: its synthesis server (scsynth) and its language (sclang), compiled to WebAssembly. They run the design's own SuperCollider files, the same ones the desktop version plays, so what you hear is the design's code rather than a translation of it. Two differences from the desktop version: one modulation source that this engine does not include (LinCongL) is replaced by a smooth random walk of the same rate and range, and video export records the sound directly instead of muxing it afterwards.
