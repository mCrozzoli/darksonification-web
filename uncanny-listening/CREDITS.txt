Uncanny Listening - credits and licences
========================================

Research: Ana Marjalizo, BA; PhD student in Aesthetic and Electronic Music and lecturer in training, Faculty of
  Information and Audiovisual Media, University of Barcelona (UB), Barcelona, Spain. Her research question, her
  classes of songs, her descriptors (dinámica, armonía, timbre, complejidad estructural) and her method (k-means
  aligned to her labels) are what this tool is built around.
Design: Miguel Angel Crozzoli. Implemented with Claude AI agents (Anthropic). Built with DarkSonification
  (https://github.com/Intelligent-Instruments-Lab/darksonification, Apache-2.0, © Miguel Angel Crozzoli and
  Intelligent Instrument Lab).
Concepts: Thor Magnusson, Sonic Writing (Bloomsbury, 2019) — the signal–symbol framework; M. A. Crozzoli et al.,
  "Dark Sonification", ICAD 2025.
Layout and legend: after Dark Re-wilding's web display (Miguel Angel Crozzoli with Alice Eldridge): the controls panel
  (information over view · lens · sound · trace) and the perceptual legend (catalogue · hear · see; DarkSonification
  manual C5 §19, modelled on Dark Skyr).

DATA
None is published. The page holds no audio and no data: each researcher opens her own folders, analysed in the
  browser; nothing is uploaded. The tests read the researcher's corpus in place on the developer's machine.

SOFTWARE PORTED TO JAVASCRIPT (no library shipped; each tested against the original, see TESTS in the repository)
DarkLibrosa (DarkSonification), summary mode, as it computed the researcher's features in January 2026.
librosa 0.11 (ISC). B. McFee, C. Raffel, D. Liang, D. P. W. Ellis, M. McVicar, E. Battenberg, O. Nieto, "librosa: Audio
  and Music Signal Analysis in Python", Proc. 14th Python in Science Conference (SciPy), 2015.
  Ported: STFT, mel filterbank (Slaney 1998, Auditory Toolbox), power_to_db, MFCC (DCT-II), chroma_stft with
  estimate_tuning / piptrack, spectral centroid, bandwidth, rolloff, flatness, contrast (D.-N. Jiang, L. Lu, H.-J. Zhang,
  J.-H. Tao, L.-H. Cai, "Music type classification by spectral contrast feature", ICME 2002), onset strength / spectral
  flux and onset detection with peak picking (S. Böck, F. Krebs, M. Schedl, "Evaluating the online capabilities of onset
  detection methods", ISMIR 2012), tempo by tempogram autocorrelation with a log-normal prior (D. P. W. Ellis, "Beat
  tracking by dynamic programming", J. New Music Research 36(1), 2007), zero-crossing rate, rms.
scikit-learn 1.7 (BSD-3-Clause). F. Pedregosa et al., "Scikit-learn: Machine Learning in Python", JMLR 12, 2011.
  Ported: MinMaxScaler; PCA (K. Pearson 1901; H. Hotelling 1933) with sklearn's svd_flip sign rule; KMeans with
  k-means++ (D. Arthur, S. Vassilvitskii, SODA 2007) and Lloyd's algorithm (S. Lloyd, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 28, 1982);
  KernelDensity (M. Rosenblatt 1956; E. Parzen 1962); LogisticRegression with L2 (D. R. Cox 1958), fitted by L-BFGS
  (D. C. Liu, J. Nocedal, Math. Programming 45, 1989); stratified k-fold cross-validation (R. Kohavi, IJCAI 1995);
  adjusted Rand index (L. Hubert, P. Arabie, J. Classification 2, 1985).
NumPy (BSD-3-Clause). C. R. Harris et al., "Array programming with NumPy", Nature 585, 2020. Ported: the legacy
  RandomState stream, Mersenne Twister MT19937 (M. Matsumoto, T. Nishimura, ACM TOMACS 8, 1998), so the clustering draws
  the same numbers as the desktop; numpy's histogram edges.
DarkSonification's align_labels: flip for two labels; Hungarian assignment otherwise (H. W. Kuhn, Naval Research
  Logistics Quarterly 2, 1955; J. Munkres, J. SIAM 5, 1957).
Statistics in the page: Cohen's d (J. Cohen, Statistical Power Analysis for the Behavioral Sciences, 1988) and η²;
  the boundary field by Nadaraya–Watson kernel regression (E. A. Nadaraya 1964; G. S. Watson 1964).
Resampling to the libsoxr "HQ" specification (libsoxr, LGPL-2.1; used as a specification only): a Kaiser-windowed sinc
  (J. F. Kaiser 1974).
FLAC decoding after the format specification (Xiph.Org; M. Q. C. van Beurden, A. Weaver, RFC 9639, 2024): our own
  decoder, core/flac.js, bit-exact against libsndfile on the test corpus.

FROM DARK MUSIC CORPUS (Miguel Angel Crozzoli, with Christian Høgh; its web engine, copied with credit, MIT)
core/mir.js, core/cqt.js (the librosa ports above), core/engine.js (Ward linkage: J. H. Ward, JASA 58, 1963; Gaussian
  mixtures by EM: A. P. Dempster, N. M. Laird, D. B. Rubin, JRSS B 39, 1977; spectral clustering: A. Y. Ng, M. I. Jordan,
  Y. Weiss, NIPS 2001; HDBSCAN: R. J. G. B. Campello, D. Moulavi, J. Sander, PAKDD 2013), core/keys.js, core/clap.js and
  core/tokenizer.js (bundled with the engine, not used by this page), the page's WAV / AIFF decoder (libsndfile-exact), and
  the Live link: the DMC Hub WebSocket contract (WS_CONTRACT v0.1) and its Max for Live devices (Cycling '74 / Ableton).
Minimal pairs (the Live template's page side, lab/LIVE_TEMPLATE.md): pairs of segments that differ in one descriptor and
  hold the others; the difference voice in Live after beats and roughness (R. Plomp, W. J. M. Levelt, "Tonal consonance
  and critical bandwidth", JASA 38, 1965).
