Dark Earth - credits and licences
========================================

Design: Miguel Angel Crozzoli. Implemented with Claude AI agents. Built with DarkSonification
  (https://github.com/Intelligent-Instruments-Lab/darksonification).

DATA
Icelandic Meteorological Office (IMO, Veðurstofa Íslands): the earthquake catalogue, the GNSS stations SKSH, SENG and
  THOB, and the MELH seismometer. Compiled and shared by Leif Karlstrom (Department of Earth Sciences, University
  of Oregon; Volcano Listening Project), with whom this design is made.
  Catalogue: IMO earthquake catalogue (Svartsengi polygon), located + magnitude-estimated by IMO
  GNSS: daily N/E/U, IMO stations SKSH, SENG, THOB (files *_060423_092426_I.csv)
  Seismometer: RK.MELH.10 HH* 100 Hz + LM* 1 Hz miniSEED, 2024-01-14 00:00-10:23 UTC, raw counts
Eruption sites: landmark coordinates © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License (ODbL),
  https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright
Lava fountains: observed fountain heights from Rhodes et al. (2026), Bulletin of Volcanology 88:89,
  doi:10.1007/s00445-026-02006-3 (supplement ESM2); the other eruptions' heights simulated with Leif Karlstrom's model.
  Fissure positions digitised from Rhodes et al. (2026), Fig. 3, or placed from IMO's reports.
Eruption dates and places: IMO's published reports, https://en.vedur.is/about-imo/news/
Fissure segment positions: the fountains' segment centroids by Leif Karlstrom (2026-11-04), approximate (150-1000 m);
  the observed eruptions' segments as digitised from Rhodes et al. (2026), Fig. 3; the simulated ones placed by Leif Karlstrom.
MELH seismometer position: approximate, from Leif Karlstrom (2026-11-04), rounded to 3 decimals (about +/-100 m).
GNSS network: IMO GNSS time series (via Leif Karlstrom); station positions: IMO GNSS browser, brunnur.vedur.is
  (https://brunnur.vedur.is/gps/browser/)
Binaural decoder (the ambisonic mode): four first-order filters derived from the MIT KEMAR HRTF measurements
  (compact set), Bill Gardner and Keith Martin, MIT Media Laboratory, 1994, https://sound.media.mit.edu/resources/KEMAR.html
  "This data is Copyright 1994 by the MIT Media Laboratory. It is provided free with no restrictions on use, provided
  the authors are cited when the data is used in any research or commercial application."
  W. G. Gardner and K. D. Martin, "HRTF measurements of a KEMAR," Journal of the Acoustical Society of America
  97(6), 3907-3908 (1995). Decoder: weighted mode-matching over all 710 measured directions, max-rE weighted,
  diffuse-field equalised.
Coastline: Natural Earth 1:10m land, public domain (https://www.naturalearthdata.com).
