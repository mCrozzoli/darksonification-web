Dark Re-wilding

Three years of the Knepp Estate soundscape, March 2023 – February 2026, read by eye and by sound.

Knepp, in West Sussex, is a former farm that has been rewilding since 2001. A microphone and a hydrophone have recorded it hour after hour since March 2023. Every recorded minute was turned into acoustic indices (numbers that describe the sound: how even it is, how much sits in the bird band, how much in the low band where engines and aircraft sit), and the air channel was listened to by BirdNET, which names the species it believes it hears. The display shows all of that by month and by day, split into four windows of the day: dawn and dusk (1.5 hours either side of sunrise and sunset), the day between, and the night.

Six views. The scatter places every month, or every day of a year, by two measures. The roses draw each month's day as a clock. The biplot lays the months out by what makes them alike. The linear view follows one month day by day. The spectrogram shows a month's energy by hour and frequency band, and the mfcc view its cepstral fingerprint. The weather lens (rain, wind, temperature) sits on top of any of them.

The sound starts at your first click or key press. Four layers read four different things. A drone and a clave (a rhythmic strike) read the acoustic indices: the measurements. An arpeggio reads the species BirdNET heard in the window: an inference, which can be wrong. When you commit a window, short pieces of the real recordings of its top bird play: the evidence. When the layers move together the picture is consistent; when they pull apart, that is the finding. Press L for the legend: hold a chip to hear one change by itself. The sound tab has a fader for every layer. Headphones help.

Data

  • Recordings: hourly files from an airborne microphone and a hydrophone at Knepp Estate (50.93° N, 0.37° W), March 2023 to February 2026. Two months (December 2023, April 2025) are missing and a few others are thin: the display fades what has little recording behind it rather than hiding it.
  • Acoustic indices: computed per minute with scikit-maad (Ulloa, J. S., et al., 2021, Methods in Ecology and Evolution 12, doi:10.1111/2041-210X.13711), then averaged by month and by day-window. One caution: the acoustic complexity index (ACI) is shown as the pipeline measured it, summed over the whole spectrogram (0–22 kHz) and not only over the analysed band (air 100 Hz – 10 kHz, water 50 Hz – 8 kHz). Part of its number therefore comes from frequencies where the recordings hold almost nothing. Read ACI here as a description of the recordings, not as a trend in the soundscape; a band-limited version is planned.
  • Species: BirdNET v2.4 on the air channel (Kahl, S., Wood, C. M., Eibl, M., & Klinck, H., 2021, Ecological Informatics 61, doi:10.1016/j.ecoinf.2021.101236). A detection is BirdNET's claim, not a confirmed record; the clips let you check it by ear.
  • Weather and sunrise/sunset: Open-Meteo (historical weather, ERA5 reanalysis; data under CC BY 4.0). The recorder's clock is UTC, and so is the display.

Sound

The sound is made by SuperCollider running inside this page: its synthesis server (scsynth) and its language (sclang), compiled to WebAssembly. They run the design's own SuperCollider files, the same ones the desktop version plays, unchanged. The part of the desktop version that turns a hover or a selection into sound messages is a Python program; it runs in this page too, unchanged, through Pyodide (CPython compiled to WebAssembly). The recordings are served as short compressed clips, decoded by the browser and handed to the synthesis server in memory.
